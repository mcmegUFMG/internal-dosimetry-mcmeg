What is SAF?

Absorbed Fraction · Specific Absorbed Fraction · Monte Carlo Calculation

1 Absorbed Fraction

The photon absorbed fraction $\phi$ is simply the fraction of the emitted photon energy by the source that is absorbed in the region of interest [1,2]. The absorbed fraction can only be determined after specifying the source and the geometry of the absorber, the photon energy, and the material medium [1].

The absorbed fraction can be expressed as the proportion of the photon energy emitted by the source that is absorbed by the target; this relationship is given by the equation below [1]:

$$\phi = \frac{\text{photon energy absorbed by target}}{\text{photon energy emitted by source}}$$

2 Specific Absorbed Fraction (SAF)

Once the absorbed fraction is obtained, it is possible to determine the Specific Absorbed Fraction (SAF) given the mass of the target; thus, the SAF is the ratio of the absorbed fraction to the mass of the target in kg⁻¹ [3]. This ratio is given by the equation below:

$$\Phi(r_T \leftarrow r_S, E_i, t) = \frac{\phi(r_T \leftarrow r_S, E_i, t)}{M(r_T, t)}$$

Where $\phi(r_T \leftarrow r_S, E_i, t)$ is the absorbed fraction (defined as the fraction of radiation energy $E_i$ emitted within the source tissue $r_S$ at time $t$ that is absorbed in the target tissue $r_T$), and $M(r_T, t)$ is the time-dependent mass of the target tissue $r_T$ in the reference individual [3].

1 SAF Calculation Method

The SAF values in this study were calculated based on the deposited energy per unit mass using the t-deposit tally available in PHITS [1]. Since the results are returned in Gy/source, which corresponds to J·kg⁻¹, it is therefore possible to calculate the SAF using the equation below:

$$\text{SAF}(r_T \leftarrow r_S) = \frac{D_{\text{PHITS},\, r_T \leftarrow r_S}}{E_{i,\, r_S}}$$

Where $D_{\text{PHITS},\, r_T \leftarrow r_S}$ is the absorbed dose obtained using the PHITS 3.34 Monte Carlo code in J·kg⁻¹, for a source-target organ pair, and $E_{i,\, r_S}$ is the energy emitted by the source organ in J.

2 Organs with Multiple Subregions

With this approach it is possible to calculate SAF values for various scenarios; however, some organs and tissues have more than one segmented region, such as the kidneys (cortex, medulla and pelvis). In these cases, the SAFs were obtained using the equation below, which represents a weighted average of the SAF values of each subregion of the corresponding organ/tissue:

$$\text{SAF organ}_{r_T \leftarrow r_S} = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i}^{n} m_i \cdot \text{SAF}_{i,\, r_i \leftarrow r_S}}{M_{\text{Organ}}}$$

Where $M_{\text{Organ}}$ is the total mass of all subregions of the organ or tissue, $m_i$ is the mass of the subregion, and $\text{SAF}_{i,\, r_T \leftarrow r_S}$ are the SAF values obtained for the subregion. This approach was used for all organs/tissues that have subregions, or bilateral organs.
